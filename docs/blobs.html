How a blob is made, and how it lives

Everything on this page is drawn by the same code as the map, live in your browser: the blobs are generated here, and the timeline comes from running the real simulation for ten minutes. Nothing is a mock-up.

01A blob is built in six steps

Every blob comes from a seed (for a real session, its id), so the same session always gets the same blob. The seed first rolls a personality, then the look is drawn from that personality, then the rules repair anything that would look wrong (section 03). Drawing is pure pixels, after Hangar: a fixed outline per agent type, scaled, and shaded as one soft gel lit from the top left, with no black outline and no mouth.

02The look follows the personality

Five traits, each from 0 to 1, decide how a blob behaves. They also tilt the dice for its look, so you can read a blob's character from its face. Below: out of 600 generated blobs, the four lowest and four highest for each trait.

03Rules repair anything that would look wrong

Random choices can clash: a cap on Gemini's ears, spots on a body too thin to show them, too many decorations at once. The rule pass fixes the clash instead of throwing the blob away, so the seed still gives the same blob. These are real repairs from the first blobs the generator made:

    The tests run 20,000 blobs through these rules and expect zero breaks; the blob lab draws thousands of them in every face and checks that every face pixel sits on the body.

    04Free time: personality, the clock, friends

    A blob whose session is finished is free, and like in Hangar it stays by its own desk: napping or playing ball in front of its slab. Now and then it walks over to a friend on its own machine for a real back-and-forth, about the friend's work, news from elsewhere, John, or the day, then comes home. The moment its session needs it again (running, waiting for you, failed), it is back at its desk.

    05How a blob chooses

    Whenever its current activity ends (15 to 40 seconds), a free blob scores every option it has: napping or playing at home, visiting a friend (at work, or free on its own plot), keeping a waiting friend company, or staying put.

    pull = how much it likes this (its personality, table above) × time of day (snacks at lunch, rest at night) × company and friends there × nearness 1 ÷ (1 + distance ÷ 8 to 18 cells) × variety × 0.6 for each time it did this lately

    It takes its top three and picks one at random, weighted by pull: sensible, never robotic. Below, the options of the first blob at the moment you hover on the timeline (or the latest one); the top three are marked ▸:

    06Real data always wins

    Personality only decides free time. The real state of each session overrides it at once: running, waiting or failed sends the blob straight to its desk, whatever it was doing; asleep (quiet a day or more) keeps it dozing beside its dark slab; a session that finishes celebrates and nearby blobs cheer; one that fails gets comfort from its two closest friends. Friendships start from real structure (same project = friends, same machine = acquaintances) and grow from chatting, playing, visits and comfort. Nothing in the world ever slows or blocks real work: Claude Code, Codex and the rest never see the blobs.
